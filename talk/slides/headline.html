Late edits along the direction ring reach the predictor's forecast. Early ones don't.

The edit reaches the forecast

Steer direction at block 22 toward a held-out value and the predictor forecasts the disk moving that way: 11–44° from target against 92° unedited and a 180° null at 136–169°. Read along the path, the forecast follows the intermediate directions on the ring route and jumps on the straight one; at matched edit size the ring route lands 8–13° closer at block 22, but not at the encoder output, where the chord does as well (12.5° vs 15.7°). Steer at block 12 or earlier and the network repairs the edit within four blocks.

Where I disagree with the paper

On the supplied clips direction decodes from block 1, and per patch it forms smoothly by block 6 with no step at her depth; on a harder render the per-patch curve rises most at points 4→6 and half-frame transfer changes sign at her depth. Mean-pooled curves were the wrong readout for the zone, as her own appendix says. No direction-specific sawtooth: that is the probe-fitting recipe.

Agreements, and one thing new

Tens of probes per variable, the steering shape (one probe fails, three to five reach the target), and a per-patch code that training builds and a random network lacks. Past the paper: direction is a stretched rank-2 ring, recoverable without labels at block 12, and at held-out arcs the spline path stays on it while the chord crosses its hollow middle in the ring plane.

Three claims I'll defend: on this stimulus availability is not the finding; direction is a rank-2 ring plus a nonlinear residual; block-22 edits reach the forecast. One I will not: that any of it transfers to richer scenes without training checkpoints and more than one hard render.
