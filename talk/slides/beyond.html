The predictor test, done twice

My first readout was blind: probes fit on real encoder tokens read the predictor's own unedited forecast about 62° off. A readout fit on the predictor's outputs (disjoint probe clips) reads it at 8.9° and 12 px, and a real counterfactual context recovers 96% of the direction change. With that readout I re-read the cached edited forecasts.

200 carrier clips × 4 held-out targets; the 180° column is 180° minus the target column by construction. At one common edit norm the spline is still 8–13° closer than the chord at point 22. At the encoder output (point 25, the predictor's input) edits reach the forecast too, but the picture is mixed: the chord is better at its own norm (12.5° vs 15.7°; 10.8° better at the chord's norm). Read along the path at 9 waypoints, the spline's forecast sweeps through the intermediate directions (mean error to the ideal intermediate 16°); the chord's stays at the source, then jumps, and its forecast radius collapses mid-path to 0.36 at large shifts (spline 0.81). Paired spline − chord: −13° overall, −31° at shifts ≥ 135°.

At point 22 the edit changes where the predictor says the disk will go, to within 11–44° of a held-out target, and along the on-ring path the forecast passes through the intermediate directions while the straight path's forecast jumps. At point 12 and earlier it does not, so geometry at the steered layer and the model's use of the edit have to be tested separately, and here they come apart.

Her essay proposes this test: steer the velocity representation and look for counterfactual changes in future predictions. Caveats come on the next slides (one stimulus, four targets in one arc, a linear readout of the pooled forecast rather than a rendered future). The reverse test (optimise a path so the forecast follows the target) found an off-ring route at the norm cap with our angle-only objective, and an unconverged run of Goodfire's own pullback recipe (shared path, top-32 replacement, L-BFGS, Hellinger target, no norm term) also moves from an on-ring start to 0.9 natural units from both the ring and the chord, with every geometry piece taken from the activation it edits; I report both as negatives. If object permanence comes up: direction decodes to 6 to 13 degrees after the disk leaves the frame, but a random-init encoder does the same, so I read it as attention mixing and not learned carrying.
