Spline versus multi-probe subspace

Multi-probe subspace (Part 1)Spline on the centroid manifold (Part 2)
What it assumesA linear subspace where the variable is read; a fixed additive stepA low-dimensional curve where the clips actually sit; a move along it
StrengthsReaches any target with 3–5 ridge probes (18 with the paper's Adam basis); works for scalars and direction alike; its edit reaches the predictor's forecast at point 22 (17°)Closes around the circle; finds its coordinate without labels at point 12 only, through my fallback (Goodfire's own label-free angle fails its periodicity test at point 12); keeps every waypoint on the ring in its plane (not closer to real clips in 64-D); expresses both routes to 180°; best forecast steer at point 22 (11°; 8–13° ahead of the chord at matched edit norm, interpolating variant; not at the encoder output)
LimitationsPasses through the ring's centre on large shifts (radius 0.14); moves the speed readout off-target; needs a basis chosen on trainEndpoints tie the line at held-out values; nothing gained on straight manifolds (speed, acceleration), extrapolation included once continued along the end tangent as the authors' code does
Failure casesA single probe never steers; a rank-2 ring rotation is not enoughThe line wins the endpoint on 1 of 16 arcs at point 22 and on the headline arc; the smoothing spline steers the forecast worse than the interpolating one; at point 12 no edit reaches the forecast (wash-out, whatever the knot order)
Same edit?Cosine between the probe step and the spline's net displacement 0.54–0.66About 40% of each edit is common to all clips; they agree on the target and disagree on the route

Neither wins outright; they are built from different things. The subspace comes from what a linear readout uses and the spline from where the clips sit. On a ring those differ. On a line they coincide, and so do the two methods.
