Methods

~50 s. The model is V-JEPA 2 ViT-L/16 at 256 by 256, frozen and in fp32. I read 26 hidden-state points per clip: the embedding, blocks 1 to 24 and the final norm, each a mean over all 2,048 tokens (the paper pools 1,568, 8 by 196). Probes are ridge regressions with the penalty picked by 5-fold cross-validation; direction is fitted to sine and cosine and scored circularly. The data are the supplied clips: one orange disk, a fixed camera, 1,500 direction clips over 64 angles, 1,536 speed and 1,536 acceleration clips. One 80/20 split per set, stratified by value, with five folds inside train for every choice. That matches the paper's C.11 split; its C.12 steering appendix uses 70/30 and its App. B uses 5-fold cross-validation. Part 1 and the Part 2 steering runs were also rerun at 70/30, and no qualitative result changes. Acceleration clips all start at rest, so acceleration, mean speed and displacement are one variable here. Three meanings of held-out are used and each result says which one it meets: held-out clips (the evaluation probe never saw the fitted clips), held-out values (steering targets that were never spline knots), and held-out renders (a textured, shaded render with a smaller disk, steered with a spline built on the supplied render). The deviations from the paper are in one table in the report: ridge instead of Adam with a parity check, 256-pixel input, 26 points, 64 directions, and the split. Where the paper gives two settings for the same step, for example stop thresholds of 0.1 and 0.05 in appendix C.11 and 0.3 and 0.1 in the Figure 22 caption, I ran both.
