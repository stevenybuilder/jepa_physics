Results · Part 2

~60 s. The whole of Part 2 on one page. Top block: the README's three variables, each at two depths. Every number is the endpoint error at a held-out target, read by the linear probe. Sources: results/p2_bakeoff_{direction_direction_L22_contiguous_rawchord, direction_direction_L12_contiguous_rawchord, speed_speed_L19_contiguous, speed_speed_L12_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L21_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L12_contiguous}.json. Two readings are on the slide. The four grey-headed columns rescale every edit to the spline's own step size (arms.*.err_probe_matched), and terracotta marks the lowest of the four. The blue column is the multi-probe, probe-QR, edit at its own natural step size (arms.probe_qr.err_probe_unmatched), and blue marks where it beats every arm at their own step sizes. Said plainly, the multi-probe method wins at its own norm, in 5 of 6 rows: 3.49 degrees at block 22 against 3.64 for the raw chord and 10.69 for the spline; 4.25 against 4.71 at block 12; 0.053 and 0.085 metres per second for speed; 0.162 metres per second squared for acceleration at block 21. The exception is acceleration at block 12, where it is 0.336 against 0.305 for the chord. It loses only when its edit is shrunk to the spline's norm, and that is where the 12.58 and 0.526 come from. A second, nonlinear reader, an MLP probe, sees every arm as poor: for direction, 19 to 33 degrees at the spline's norm and 17 to 33 at own norms, against 86 to 92 unedited; for speed, 0.39 to 0.60 metres per second for every arm (arms.*.err_mlp_{matched,unmatched}). So "lands on target" is partly a statement about the linear reader. Among the geometric arms, on the ring a straight edit lands at least as close as the spline at the held-out endpoint. The spline's advantage is the path: its waypoints stay on the ring (readout radius 0.86 against 0.63). At block 22 the interpolating spline is also the edit the forecast follows, 11.3 degrees from the target against 92.1 unedited (results/session2_predictor_native_readout.json per_layer.22.{spline,zero}.dir_err_to_target). On the lines the spline adds nothing over the chord; the terracotta there marks a tie. Speed edits at block 22 move the forecast's speed code 62 percent of the way, with spline and chord equal, but the forecast disk does not speed up (results/session3_speed_predictor.json). Acceleration was not run through the predictor. Bottom block: what the shape view buys. The velocity sheet is a cone. One 2-D edit sets direction to 2.9 degrees, against 6.9 for the 1-D ring, and leaks 0.03 metres per second into speed (results/p5_velocity_sheet.json). The time step traces a curve, not a line. Steering along it lands 0.106 steps off, and the raw chord 0.082, against 2.43 unedited. It leaves the decoded disk position unchanged, 0.479 against 0.477 metres at block 12 (results/p5_time_manifold.json steer.{12,22}.summary). An untrained copy also decodes the step, at R squared 0.97. The time-step code advances 1.00 per step at every speed, where a distance code would give 0.38 and 1.63. The untrained copy is speed-invariant too on the speed set, fast over slow 0.90 to 1.07, but its slope is 0.65 to 0.77 and it has no shared curve: held-out variance explained is 0.81 against 0.009 at block 22 (results/p5_time_manifold_controls.json). Training adds the unit slope and the curve. At a fixed frame rate, the code counts frames and cannot be told apart from seconds. The time-rescaled renders are speed changes in disguise, so that test is decoded only (results/CLOCK_NOTES.md). Relative speed of two disks is decoded, never steered: a linear probe for the magnitude of v1 minus v2 reaches R squared 0.945 to 0.976 at blocks 13 to 22, against 0.19 to 0.22 for the untrained copy (results/p5_relational_motion.json beyond_composition.sources.*.targets.rel_speed.lin_acts.test_r2; headline.best_by_target.abs_v_rel 0.969 against 0.204). The untrained copy's single-disk readouts, composed, reach about 0.92, so the claim is that V-JEPA 2 carries an identity-blind relative-speed code directly, not that the untrained network cannot get there. Binding: disk tokens carry 0.88 of the forecast's move at block 12 and 0.22 at block 22 (results/p5_token_patching.json). So the code is bound to the object through the emergence zone and spread across the frame where the predictor reads it.
