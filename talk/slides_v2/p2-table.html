Results · Part 2

~60 s. The whole of Part 2 on one page. Top block: the README's three variables, each at two depths, and every number is the endpoint error at a held-out target, read by the matched probe reader (results/p2_bakeoff_{direction_direction_L22_contiguous_rawchord, direction_direction_L12_contiguous_rawchord, speed_speed_L19_contiguous, speed_speed_L12_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L21_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L12_contiguous}.json, arms.{spline, chord, chord_raw, probe_qr}.err_probe_matched). Terracotta marks the lowest error in the row. The spline is the smoothing spline, the chord runs between smoothed knots, and the raw chord runs between raw class means. The honest reading. On the ring, a straight edit already lands at least as close as the spline at the held-out endpoint: the smoothed chord at block 22, 7.45 against 10.69 degrees, and at block 12 the raw chord, 6.38 against 9.73, with the probe-QR multi-probe edit lower still on this matched reader, 5.62. The earlier comparison slide uses the unmatched reader (4.2, 4.7, 9.7), so the ordering is the same but the values differ. The spline's advantage on the ring is the path: its waypoints stay on the ring (readout radius 0.86 against 0.63), and at block 22 the interpolating spline is the edit the forecast follows, 11.3 degrees from the target against 92.1 unedited (results/session2_predictor_native_readout.json per_layer.22.{spline,zero}.dir_err_to_target). On the lines, speed and acceleration, the spline adds nothing over the chord: they differ by at most 0.008, a tie, and the terracotta there marks a tie, not a win. Through the predictor, speed edits at block 22 move the forecast's speed code 62 percent of the way to the target, with spline and chord equal, but the forecast disk does not move faster (results/session3_speed_predictor.json predictor.22.own.{spline,chord}.direct_R_speed). Acceleration was not run through the predictor. Bottom block: this is what the shape view buys. The velocity sheet is a cone, and one 2-D edit sets direction to 2.9 degrees against 6.9 for the 1-D ring while leaking 0.03 metres per second into speed (results/p5_velocity_sheet.json layers.22.steer.direction.summary). The time step within a clip traces a curve, not a line (a line in t explains 0.15 of held-out variance). Steering along it lands 0.106 steps off, the raw chord 0.082, unedited 2.43 (results/p5_time_manifold.json steer.22.summary). But an untrained copy also decodes the step at R squared 0.97 (results/p5_time_manifold_controls.json speed/random/timepool.decode.22.test.t_r2), so the step may be the encoder's token-time position code. The time-step code is speed-invariant: held-out slow, mid and fast clips advance 1.00 per step, where a distance code would give 0.38, 1.00 and 1.63. At a fixed frame rate it counts frames, and it cannot be told apart from elapsed seconds. The time-rescaled renders are speed changes in disguise, so that test is confounded with speed and only decoded (results/CLOCK_NOTES.md). Relative speed of two disks is decoded, never steered: a nonlinear probe reaches R squared 0.94 to 0.98 at blocks 13 to 22, against a median of 0.52 for the untrained copy (results/p5_relational_motion.json beyond_composition.sources.*[p].targets.rel_speed.mlp_acts.test_r2). Binding: swapping disk tokens carries 0.88 of the forecast's move at block 12 and 0.22 at block 22 (results/p5_token_patching.json). So the code is bound to the object through the emergence zone and spread across the frame where the predictor reads it.
