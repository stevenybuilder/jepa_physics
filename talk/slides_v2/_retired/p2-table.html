~50 s. One row per variable, three questions in words. Shape: direction is a ring; speed and acceleration are straight lines; direction and speed together form a two-dimensional sheet whose ring radius grows with speed and then flattens; the within-clip time step traces a curve, not a line (a line in t explains 0.15 of held-out variance, the curve 0.82). Does a straight edit do the job? For direction, the straight edit between raw centroids leaves the ring mid-path, but at held-out targets it lands as close as the curved edit or closer: 4.7 against 9.7 degrees at block 12, 3.6 against 10.7 at block 22 on the headline arc, each at its own norm. For speed and acceleration the spline adds nothing over the chord; they are lines. On the sheet, one 2-D edit lands 4.3 degrees from a held-out cell against 8.7 for two 1-D edits in turn (results/p5_velocity_sheet.json layers.22.steer.joint.summary). For time, the straight edit lands 0.082 steps off against 0.106 for the spline, 2.43 unedited. Does the forecast obey? For direction, only edits at block 22: the interpolating spline's forecast lands 11.3 degrees from the target against 92.1 unedited (results/session2_predictor_native_readout.json per_layer.22.{spline,zero}.dir_err_to_target.mean). For speed, block-22 edits move the forecast's speed code, R 0.62, spline equal to chord, but the forecast disk does not speed up (results/session3_speed_predictor.json). Acceleration through the predictor and the sheet through the predictor are running; no numbers yet. Time was not run through the predictor. The within-clip time code counts frames at every speed on the speed set, 0.99 to 1.00 per step at block 22, where a distance counter predicts 0.21 and 1.59; the untrained copy gives 0.71 to 0.77. The full numeric table, every arm at the spline's norm and the paper's probe method at its own norm, is the appendix slide after the close, for discussion. Framing update: the spline column is our first FITPACK smoother. Endpoint diagnosis (results/p2_endpoint_diagnosis.json, REPORT §4.3): most of the endpoint loss was our own spline, a FITPACK smoother fitted per coordinate, not the method. Running the authors' own interpolating spline code (causalab, lambda 0) on our knots turns the block-22 headline arc from +7.05 degrees in the straight edit's favour to -0.48 in the curve's (decomposition.L22_labels.headline_seed0.baseline_gap_ours_smooth_minus_raw_chord.value; .b_smoothing.interp_causalab_lam0.value). Over 16 arcs the paper's interpolating spline is within about 1 degree of the straight edit, +0.84 at block 22 and +1.56 at block 12 (decomposition.L22_labels.arcs16.b_smoothing.interp_causalab_lam0.value; L12_labels.arcs16 same key). A cross-validated smoother wins on 16 of 16 arcs by 0.45 and 0.85 degrees (decomposition.{L22,L12}_labels.arcs16.b_smoothing.reinsch_lam_cv.{value,n_arm_better}); that arm is exploratory. Norm is not the cause. The ring's bend across a 45-degree gap is real but smaller than the held-out centroids' own noise, and a linear probe reads straight-edit points almost exactly by construction. So at the endpoint the two are indistinguishable; the curve's advantage is the path. We found this by running the authors' code on our data.
