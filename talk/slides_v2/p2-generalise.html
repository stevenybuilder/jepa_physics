The spline, the matched chord and the raw chord are built on the supplied render's knot clips, then applied to 300 held-out clips of the textured render, steering into the same held-out 45-degree arc. The readout is a probe fitted on textured-render clips disjoint from the steered ones; read with the supplied-render probe instead, the same block-22 edits appear 67 degrees off, so the readout must be render-matched (ledger #226). Spline minus matched chord at the endpoint is minus 0.03 degrees, interval minus 0.42 to 0.32, at block 12, a tie, and plus 3.83, interval 2.82 to 4.77, at block 22, where the chord is ahead. The raw chord between unsmoothed centroids is ahead of the spline by 4.31 degrees at block 12 and 4.85 at block 22, as on the supplied render. Averaged over shifts of 15 to 165 degrees the spline lands 23.7 degrees from the target at block 12 and 14.0 at block 22, the raw chord 19.4 and 9.2. Path metrics keep the spline's advantage in the ring plane at block 12 (minimum readout radius plus 0.016, interval 0.007 to 0.024). What transfers, then, is the geometry: a curve fitted on one render moves the textured render's activations to the right direction, so the ring is a property of the code, not of one rendering. What does not transfer is the endpoint ordering in the spline's favour, which never held on this design. The object-versus-scene binding index and the two-disk relational decoding are running on CPU as I speak and go in the report; the binding index by depth is already visible in the logs, near zero at every block, meaning the direction code is read as well from background patches as from the disk's own patches.
