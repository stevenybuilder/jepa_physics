This is the README's requested comparison of spline steering with the Part 1 multi-probe method. Multi-probe: a linear subspace where the variable is read, a fixed additive step; it reaches the target with a handful of probes and works for scalars and direction alike, but a single probe with a plain edit fails (78 degrees) and it needs labelled probes. Chord: the straight line between two class means; it lands closest among the geometric arms at the endpoint but crosses the ring's empty centre mid-path (radius 0.23 at the midpoint for large shifts). Spline: a curve through every class mean; it keeps every waypoint on the ring in the ring plane and gives the best forecast at block 22 (11.3 degrees), but its smoothed knots pull the endpoint off the held-out class means (9.7 degrees), and on straight manifolds, speed and acceleration, it adds nothing. About 40 percent of the spline's edit is common to all source values; the probe step and the spline's net displacement have cosine 0.54 to 0.66. This is the negative result. The comparison baseline in the paper's appendix A.9 is the straight chord between the raw class means at the two ends. Run with our matched-support edit, it lands closer to a held-out target than the smoothing spline: 4.7 against 9.7 degrees at point 12 and 3.6 against 10.7 at point 22 on the headline arc, with clip-bootstrap gaps of plus 5.0, interval 3.9 to 6.1, and plus 7.0, interval 6.0 to 8.1. The headline arc is the extreme case at point 12 and the second largest at point 22. Over the 16 arcs the chord's lead is 1.3 plus or minus 1.8 degrees at point 12, with the spline ahead on 3, and 2.3 plus or minus 2.0 at point 22, ahead on none. The figure shows why on this arc: the held-out class means, the open circles, sit close to the chord, and the smoothing spline, fitted without them through smoothed knots, bends away. The spline only ties the chord drawn between its own smoothed knots, which was our line arm until a parity audit against the paper's code. For speed and acceleration, which are straight, the spline adds nothing; in extrapolation our smoothing spline trails the chord by 0.02 to 0.06, while the authors' interpolating arm is better on speed and worse on acceleration.
