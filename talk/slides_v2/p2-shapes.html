Results · Part 2

~60 s. Source: results/p5_velocity_sheet.json, layers.22.steer and layers.22.geometry. The velocity sheet is a 2-D manifold fitted to the (speed, direction) class means, and steering targets are held-out cells the sheet never saw. Steering direction along the sheet lands 2.881 degrees from the target, against 6.900 for the 1-D ring spline and 4.385 for the raw chord (direction.summary.*.endpoint_err), and leaks 0.034 metres per second into speed against 0.081 for the ring and 0.051 for the chord (offtarget_change). Steering speed along the sheet is as accurate as the 1-D speed line, 0.108 against 0.121 metres per second, and moves direction by 1.235 degrees against 3.041 for the line and 11.448 for the chord. One joint sheet edit to a held-out cell lands 4.334 degrees and 0.110 metres per second off, against 8.685 degrees and 0.141 for two sequential 1-D edits (joint.summary). The gap sheet minus chord on direction endpoint error is minus 1.504 degrees, interval minus 1.901 to minus 1.106. Geometry: the ring's noise-corrected radius grows from 4.9 to 11.4 across the speed bins (radius_by_speed_bin.radius_noise_corrected), a cone fits held-out cells with R squared 0.827 against 0.789 for a cylinder, with a thin-plate sheet at 0.834 and a noise ceiling of 0.902; the random-init encoder gives cone 0.29 against a ceiling of 0.54. Point 12 shows the same ordering: sheet 4.208 degrees against ring 6.674 and chord 4.629, speed leakage 0.050 against 0.182 and 0.044. Why this matters: Goodfire's paper shows factored control on a synthetic grid; here it is two physical variables of a video world model, and the finding is that the encoder keeps them as separate axes of one surface, so a controller can set either without disturbing the other. The elapsed-time result on the next slide adds a third variable to that picture. The position sheet (results/p2_sheet_speed_L12.json, _L19.json) gives no path advantage and stays in the report.
