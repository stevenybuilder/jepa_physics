~60 s. The whole of Part 2 on one page. Top block: the README's three variables, each at two depths. Every number is the endpoint error at a held-out target, read by the linear probe. Sources: results/p2_bakeoff_{direction_direction_L22_contiguous_rawchord, direction_direction_L12_contiguous_rawchord, speed_speed_L19_contiguous, speed_speed_L12_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L21_contiguous, acceleration_acceleration_L12_contiguous}.json. Two readings are on the slide. The four grey-headed columns rescale every edit to the spline's own step size (arms.*.err_probe_matched), and terracotta marks the lowest of the four. The exception is acceleration at block 12, where it is 0.336 against 0.305 for the chord. It loses only when its edit is shrunk to the spline's norm, and that is where the 12.58 and 0.526 come from. A second, nonlinear reader, an MLP probe, sees every arm as poor: for direction, 19 to 33 degrees at the spline's norm and 17 to 33 at own norms, against 86 to 92 unedited; for speed, 0.39 to 0.60 metres per second for every arm (arms.*.err_mlp_{matched,unmatched}). The spline's advantage is the path: its waypoints stay on the ring (readout radius 0.86 against 0.63). At block 22 the interpolating spline is also the edit the forecast follows, 11.3 degrees from the target against 92.1 unedited (results/session2_predictor_native_readout.json per_layer.22.{spline,zero}.dir_err_to_target). On the lines the spline adds nothing over the chord; the terracotta there marks a tie. Speed edits at block 22 move the forecast's speed code 62 percent of the way, with spline and chord equal, but the forecast disk does not speed up (results/session3_speed_predictor.json). Acceleration has no predictor number yet. Bottom block: what the shape view buys. Direction and speed form one sheet, close to a cylinder whose radius grows with speed. One joint edit lands 4.3 degrees from a held-out cell against 7.0 for two 1-D edits built from interpolating splines; direction alone 2.9 against 4.1 for the interpolating ring; about two thirds of the gain is estimation, one third the radius scaling (results/p5_velocity_sheet_v2.json results.22.block2.{joint,direction}.own.summary). Through the predictor 27.6 against 31.5 degrees (p5_velocity_sheet_predictor.json predictor.own.{sheet,seq1d_interp}.dir_err_deg). The time step traces a curve, not a line. Steering along it lands 0.106 steps off, and the raw chord 0.082, against 2.43 unedited. It leaves the decoded disk position unchanged, 0.479 against 0.477 metres at block 12 (results/p5_time_manifold.json steer.{12,22}.summary). An untrained copy also decodes the step, at R squared 0.97. Through the predictor a +2-step edit advances the forecast −0.21 of the real displacement at block 22, against 1.15 for real later frames (results/p5_time_predictor.json slide_numbers). The time-step code advances 1.00 per step at every speed, where a distance code would give 0.38 and 1.63. The untrained copy is speed-invariant too on the speed set, fast over slow 0.90 to 1.07, but its slope is 0.65 to 0.77 and it has no shared curve: held-out variance explained is 0.81 against 0.009 at block 22 (results/p5_time_manifold_controls.json). Training adds the unit slope and the curve. At a fixed frame rate, the code counts frames and cannot be told apart from seconds. The time-rescaled renders are speed changes in disguise, so that test is decoded only (results/CLOCK_NOTES.md). Relative speed of two disks is decoded, never steered: a linear probe for the magnitude of v1 minus v2 reaches R squared 0.945 to 0.976 at blocks 13 to 22, against 0.19 to 0.22 for the untrained copy (results/p5_relational_motion.json beyond_composition.sources.*.targets.rel_speed.lin_acts.test_r2; headline.best_by_target.abs_v_rel 0.969 against 0.204). The untrained copy's single-disk readouts, composed, reach about 0.92, so the claim is that V-JEPA 2 carries an identity-blind relative-speed code directly, not that the untrained network cannot get there. Binding: disk tokens carry 0.88 of the forecast's move at block 12 and 0.22 at block 22 (results/p5_token_patching.json). So the code is bound to the object through and after the Physics Emergence Zone and spread across the frame where the predictor reads it. Norm is not the cause. The ring's bend across a 45-degree gap is real but smaller than the held-out centroids' own noise, and a linear probe reads straight-edit points almost exactly by construction. So at the endpoint the two are indistinguishable; the curve's advantage is the path. Direction rows now come from results/p2_bakeoff_unified_16arc.json table..err_end. (16 held-out arcs, 8 targets, 48 clips each, each edit at its own size); the speed and acceleration rows are still the earlier per-variable bake-offs at the spline's norm. At the straight edit's size the paper's spline reads 17.0 at block 22 and our probe-subspace steer 22.8, because they move 1.4 and 1.5 times as far; at block 12 the paper's spline leaves the ring outward on the label-free angle, 34.5.
