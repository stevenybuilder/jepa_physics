Methods

~50 s. Part 1 follows the paper's three steps in order: layer-wise probes, iterative nullspace probing, and multi-probe subspace steering. Part 2 applies Goodfire's recipe: PCA to 64 dimensions on the training clips, one centroid per label value, a periodic spline for direction and a natural spline for the scalars, and steering by moving along the spline. The design of the steering test matters. At a value the spline saw, the chord and the curve end at the same point, so an endpoint readout cannot tell linear from spline steering. At a held-out value the endpoints differ and the paths differ all the way along. So the main design holds out a contiguous 45-degree arc of 8 directions and steers into it, repeated at 16 arc positions at each of blocks 12 and 22. Three disjoint clip sets: 632 knot clips build the spline, 480 probe clips fit the evaluation probe, 300 test clips are steered. Both arms edit the same PCA-64 subspace and keep each clip's off-subspace residual. Controls: a dose-matched line, projected and reflected arms, 20 endpoint-matched random curves, 20 shuffled-centroid curves, and a BF16 repeat. The verdict rule was frozen before the arc sweep and applied unchanged to all 32 runs. The judge is never the probe that built the edit: an evaluation probe on disjoint clips, agreement with real clips at the target value, and the downstream test, which edits the encoder, runs V-JEPA 2's predictor, and reads the direction of its forecast with a probe fitted on predictor outputs from other clips. That readout gets the unedited forecast to 8.9 degrees, and a real counterfactual context recovers 96 percent of the direction change, so it is sensitive enough to judge an edit.
