Iterative nullspace probing at point 9, the paper's layer 8. With the count nested inside each training fold it takes 37 probes to bring direction to the stop line, 39 for speed and 41 for acceleration; scoring on the test split, as the paper's protocol does, gives 46, 45 and 47. Removing the same number of random dimensions leaves R squared at 0.980. Two things did not reproduce cleanly: whether speed needs fewer probes than direction depends on the stop rule and the probe recipe (fewer under C.11's thresholds in 7 of 8 cells, more under the Figure 22 caption's in all 8), and there is no direction-specific sawtooth under ridge or under the paper's Adam recipe. What the count measures: at points 9, 12 and 22 the literal count is 37, 64 and 88, but after whitening one two-output probe does the whole job at every point. The code is a rank-2 plane, the sine and cosine of the angle, stretched so that an unwhitened probe sequence needs tens of rounds to exhaust it. Planted controls decide the reading: a sheared ring inflates the literal count but not the whitened one, three planted copies do not inflate it, and a planted third harmonic is detected. Erasing the plane with LEACE drops a ridge probe to zero, but an MLP still reads 0.37 to 0.82 after erasure, and that nonlinear residual grows toward the output. Step 3 reproduces: five ridge probes steer to 8.7 degrees, a random basis of the same rank to 57.2, and with the paper's Adam probe sequence it takes 18 probes to get under 10 degrees, against about 12 degrees at around 20 in the paper. The addition: an edit along one probe's own axis fails, 78 degrees, consistent with the paper's more than 80. Weight the same probe by the activation covariance, which takes the edit out of the probe's plane, and it reaches 3.2 degrees. Random two-dimensional planes reach a median of 4.2 degrees, not significantly worse on target error, p = 0.24. What the learned probe buys is specificity: 0.03 metres per second of off-target speed change against 4.2 for random planes. So the many-probe requirement is a property of the Euclidean edit. This does not contradict the paper; its edit is Euclidean and that result reproduces.
