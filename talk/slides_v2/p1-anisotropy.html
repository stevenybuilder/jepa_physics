At the paper's layer it takes 37 probes to erase direction, while removing the same number of random directions leaves R squared at 0.980. After whitening, a single two-output probe does the whole job, but that holds for any linearly readable 2-D target by construction: all four planted controls give 1. After erasing that plane a nonlinear reader still recovers 0.37, 0.41 and 0.82 R squared at blocks 9, 12 and 22 (p1b_dims_four_ways.json variables.direction.layers[].leace.mlp_r2_after). Steering shows the same thing. An edit along one probe's own axis fails at 78 degrees. The same probe weighted by the activation covariance reaches 3.2 degrees, and random planes reach a median of 4.2, so what the learned probe buys is specificity, not reach. The count measures the covariance's shape, not how much information there is. At the predictor, on the ridge basis at block 22, the nullspace directions found early each move the forecast more (Spearman minus 0.60), and directions past about 16 are no better than random single edits of the same norm.
