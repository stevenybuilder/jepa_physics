~40 s. Three claims I would defend. One: the per-patch code is training-selective; V-JEPA 2's mean per-patch R squared is 0.96 at block 6 while a random network's never passes 0.39, and on a harder render the jump appears at the paper's depth on all three seeds. Two: every variable needs tens of probes at the paper's layer, far outside a random-removal band, and the steering count reproduces with the paper's own probe recipe, 18 probes to reach 10 degrees. Three: direction lies on a ring, and at held-out directions the spline path stays on the ring while the chord cuts across its hollow in the ring plane. One I would not defend: that any of this transfers to Kubric-scale scenes without checkpoint experiments and more than one hard render. Two honest limits on the thesis: at the predictor's input, block 25, the raw chord leads the spline at matched norm, and for speed, a straight variable, the two forecasts are indistinguishable; the geometry buys something only where the code is curved, and only along the path. Once you know the model has a variable, the next question is what it does with it; on this data that depends on where you edit, and the variable's geometry decides how you have to edit.
