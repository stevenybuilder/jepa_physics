Closing question

Can a world model run a counterfactual we write into it, at the interface its predictor reads?

Joseph's frame: the world model is the network's own state variables and dynamics, and interpretability asks how they combine to produce behaviour.

We found those variables readable from the middle of the encoder but consumed only at its exit. That exit is where a heading, a position or a coming contact would have to be written for the forecast to answer a what-if.

If it works, a world model becomes steerable one physical variable at a time, and a robot's plan can be checked by editing its state and reading the forecast. If it fails, the predictor re-derives physics from pixels, and the readable variables are not the ones it computes with.

Sources: Joseph, "World models and interpretability are two sides of the same coin" (soniajoseph.ai): "The world model is the internal state variables and dynamics learned by the foundation model itself"; "what are the network's internal variables, and how do they combine through the network's internal circuitry to generate behavior?"; found and headline slides. ~60 s. Our result narrows her question to one interface: the variables exist early, the predictor reads them late, so a counterfactual must be written at the exit, or on the object's tokens.
