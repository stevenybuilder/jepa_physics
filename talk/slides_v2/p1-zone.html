The terracotta curve is decodability per patch position; the navy curve is a probe trained on one half of the frame and tested on the other, so it measures whether the code is the same wherever the disk is. The supplied clips are deliberately simple, so I rendered a harder set: a textured floor, shading and a smaller disk, three render seeds with 392 clips each. Under the declared onset rule, the first point at 90 percent of the maximum with a 200-draw bootstrap, the per-patch onset is 9, 8 and 8 on the three seeds, with intervals 9 to 9, 8 to 9 and 8 to 9: the paper's depth. The curve's largest rise is between points 4 and 6 on every seed, plus 0.24 to 0.29, and only plus 0.07 to 0.09 across 8 to 9; the onset lands at 8 or 9 because the curve keeps climbing, and the sigmoid inflection is at 5.7 to 5.9. A random-init network on the same clips sits at 0.52 to 0.62 from point 6 on. The half-frame probe is 0.7 at point 1, is negative from about point 4 to point 8 (seeds 1 and 2 still slightly positive at block 4, 0.16 and 0.15, all three negative by block 7) and jumps back at 9 on every seed, a miscalibration between halves rather than a mirror flip; the random network never shows that shape. So the agreement is partial: the onset lands at the paper's depth under the 90 percent rule, but the largest rise is over points 4 to 6, and transfer is not absent before the zone. On the supplied clips there is no such step. The paper-layout render gives onset 6. Why this matters: the zone is a change in kind, from many local codes to one frame-wide code, which is the property a downstream controller would need. Limits: 10 sampled points on seed 0 and 13 on seeds 1 and 2, so early onsets are upper bounds; 7 shared start positions per seed.
