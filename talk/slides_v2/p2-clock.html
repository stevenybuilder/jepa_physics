Results · Part 2

~55 s. Source: results/p5_time_manifold.json (clock, geometry, decode, steer), direction set, block 12 unless stated. Within a clip each of the 8 time steps has its own pooled feature. Subtract the clip's mean over steps and the 8 residuals trace a path. Elapsed time is decodable from that residual with test R squared 1.00 from block 8 on (decode, t_r2), and the random-init encoder also decodes it at 0.97, so decodability alone says little; the shape and the rate do. Geometry: a line in t explains 0.15 of held-out clips' residual variance, the step centroids and the spline through them 0.77 (geometry.heldout_var_explained), and the bend is 61 times the centroid noise, so the time path is a curve, not a line. The clock test: fit a time spline through the step centroids of mid-speed knot clips, then read each held-out clip's steps as the nearest point on it. On slow, mid and fast held-out clips the coordinate advances 1.003, 0.998 and 0.996 per step (clock.spline.*.slope.mean). If the coordinate tracked distance travelled, an odometer, the slow band would advance 0.38 per step and the fast band 1.63 (odometer_prediction). The untrained copy gives 0.51, 0.58 and 0.94, neither clock nor odometer. The same holds at blocks 8, 19 and 22 and on the speed and acceleration sets. Steering: moving a step's residual along the time spline sets the decoded time to within 0.10 step, against 2.43 unedited, and the raw chord does the same, 0.09 (steer.12.summary), as a curve with small sagitta predicts. Why this matters: time is the variable a next-frame predictor must carry, it was not among the paper's three, and the code is speed-invariant, which is what a clock is. A control with a static disk and a speed-rescaled render (results/p5_clock_test.json and the time-controls run) is in the report.
