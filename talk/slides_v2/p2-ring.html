~60 s. Each dot is the mean activation of the training clips at one of the 64 directions, at block 12, projected onto the ring plane. The curve is the fitted periodic smoothing spline, sampled densely in angle order. The angle read off the ring without labels, as Goodfire's text does with the top two components, has a circular correlation of minus 0.98 with the true direction at point 12, and it holds on every arc's knot clips. At point 22 the label-free angle fails on the knot clips for the headline arc and 12 of 16 arcs, so point 22 steers on the labels angle; label-free recovery is my extension and works at point 12 only. Fitted in its own plane the ring is an ellipse, axis ratio 0.68 to 0.89 from point 6 on, bent out of plane by a saddle. Speed and acceleration centroids lie on nearly straight curves: the sagitta over a held-out span is 0.03 to 0.24 of the centroid noise (from the steering runs' sagitta_per_target). The ring's radius grows with speed, so speed and direction together form a cone, which is the next slide. One consequence: in the ring's plane a straight step from 0 toward 180 degrees passes through the centre, where no real clip lives. Held-out clips fill the ring, the largest angular gap is 2.9 degrees, and the chord midpoint for a 180-degree step sits at radius 0.09. Why the shape matters: an additive probe edit and a chord both move in straight lines, and on a ring a straight line leaves the set of states the model has ever produced; on a line it does not. So the geometry predicts where the spline should help, direction, and where it cannot, speed and acceleration, and that prediction is what the steering runs test.
