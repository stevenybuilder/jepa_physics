~65 s. Sources: results/p1a_direction_direction_meanpool.json, p1a_direction_direction_meanpool_random.json, p1a_perpatch_direction_vjepa2.json, p1a_perpatch_direction_random.json. Pooled probes first, the dashed lines. Pooled over all tokens, every variable is decodable from the first block. Direction reaches a cross-validated R squared of 0.875 at block 1, speed 0.983 and acceleration 0.977. The peaks are 0.991 at point 22, 0.994 at point 19 and 0.992 at point 21, and there is no late decline. The grey line is the same probe on a randomly initialised ViT-L: 0.848 at block 1, rising to 0.868. Random ReLU features of the trajectory itself reach 0.875. The paper describes speed and acceleration appearing early and direction at about one third of depth. Pooled, I see the direction onset at block 2 under every fold grouping and also at the paper's 392-clip scale. So on this stimulus a pooled probe score tells you almost nothing about what training did: a single high-contrast disk on a flat floor is linearly recoverable from almost any spatially pooled features, which is what the random-feature control shows. Appendix C.5 of the paper puts the change in per-patch probes. Then per patch, the solid lines. Each patch position gets its own probe and I average the test R squared over positions. V-JEPA 2 goes from 0.56 at block 1 to 0.89 at block 5 and 0.96 at block 6; the onset under the declared rule is block 5, bootstrap interval 5 to 5. The random network pools to 0.86 to 0.88 at every depth but its mean per-patch R squared never gets above 0.38; its best single position reaches 0.76 to 0.86 and its half-frame transfer stays at 0.61 to 0.74. That is the fragmented local signal that pooling adds up. V-JEPA 2 leaves that regime within six blocks. VideoMAE matches V-JEPA 2 on every pooled measure, so pooled availability is largely architectural, and the per-patch code is what training adds beyond a random-init copy; VideoMAE's per-patch code is being measured. Caveats: per-patch features are averaged over the 8 time steps, and the half-frame test uses one pooled probe per half.
