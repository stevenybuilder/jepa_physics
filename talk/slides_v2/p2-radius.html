The x axis runs from the source heading to the target; the y axis is how much heading code is left, 1 being a real clip. For large shifts, 135 to 180 degrees, the straight edit falls to 0.23 halfway: it passes through states with almost no heading. The curve stays near a real clip's level the whole way, on all 16 held-out arcs. Why held-out targets: at a value the curve saw, both edits end at the same point. At the endpoint the straight edit still wins, by 0.84 degrees at block 22 and 1.56 at block 12 with the authors' own spline code; at block 22 that is down from 2.33 with our first smoother, at block 12 up from 0.81. So the curve's advantage is the path, and at block 22 the forecast follows that path while the straight edit's jumps. What would change our mind: a rendered future in which the straight edit's intermediate frames look as coherent.
